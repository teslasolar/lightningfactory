How This Works

An AC power line creates a time-varying magnetic field around it. By Faraday's law, this changing field induces an electric field in any conductive loop — including your body.


The induced current depends on:


• Magnetic field strength — falls off as 1/r from the line

• Body cross-section — modeled as ~0.1 m² loop area

• Body impedance — roughly 500Ω at power-line frequencies

• Frequency — higher frequency = more induced EMF


The magnetic field from a long straight conductor: B = µ₀I / (2πr)

Induced EMF: V = 2πf · B · A

Body current: I = V / Z
