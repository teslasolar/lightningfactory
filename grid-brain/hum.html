Why 55 Hz?

55 Hz sits between the two grid frequencies — it's not 50 Hz and not 60 Hz. It falls squarely in the gamma band of human brain oscillations.

If you wanted to study or experience a gamma-range frequency without interference from the power grid, 55 Hz is the natural choice. It's far enough from both 50 and 60 Hz that a notch filter won't touch it, and grid harmonics won't mask it.


Gamma oscillations (30–100 Hz) are associated with:

• Focused attention and concentration

• Working memory binding

• Sensory integration across brain regions

• Conscious awareness


Whether an acoustic tone at 55 Hz can entrain neural gamma is debated. Some studies on "gamma entrainment" use 40 Hz audio or light flicker, but the mechanism (if any) likely involves auditory steady-state response (ASSR) rather than direct electromagnetic coupling.
